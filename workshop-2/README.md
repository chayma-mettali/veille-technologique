## Question
What are the potential research gaps in heart failure diagnosis using AI models based on ECG?

## Prompt envoyé à l'IA
Base your answer on these three sources:

1. Official source: Armoundas AA et al., "Use of Artificial Intelligence in Improving Outcomes in Heart Disease: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association", Circulation, 2024, DOI: 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001201

2. Scientific article: Chepkunov D et al., "Artificial intelligence-augmented electrocardiogram for heart failure diagnosis and risk prediction: a narrative review", Journal of Medical Artificial Intelligence, April 2026, DOI: 10.21037/jmai-2025-178

3. Newsletter: Cardiovascular Business, "AI's growing influence in cardiology | New heart failure implant shows potential | FDA clears advanced technologies", 17 March 2026, https://cardiovascularbusiness.com/newsletter/2026-03-17/ais-growing-influence-cardiology-new-heart-failure-implant-shows-potential-fda-clears-advanced

Compare the three sources, point out contradictions, and indicate the reliability level of each one.

## Réponse de l'IA

First, according to the American Heart Association statement, most AI models still lack prospective validation, meaning they haven’t been tested on real patients going forward in time, and their real-world impact is not well studied. Second, the same statement warns about bias and generalizability, so models need testing on large, diverse populations, otherwise they could worsen health inequities. Third, it also mentions limited ECG data for training, and missing regulatory, legal and ethical frameworks.

Fourth, the 2026 narrative review says AI-ECG models perform well across different types of heart failure, but that conventional ECG reading has limited sensitivity, so the gap is moving these models from research into routine clinical use. Finally, the Cardiovascular Business newsletter shows the market is moving fast, with more than 200 FDA-cleared cardiology AI tools, but cardiology societies say AI still has a long way to go before wide adoption.

On reliability, the AHA statement is the strongest source, the review is solid but I only read its abstract, and the newsletter is useful for news but may lean toward industry.
